Guide: what to do, and why?

Goal: find out whether the dice on images of the camera board are large enough to recognise the symbols. If you cannot read them reliably with your own eyes, no model can either. Then we first have to change distance or resolution before putting time into rendering and training.

How it works:

  1. Throw 5 dice into the tray.
  2. Hold the camera board the way you would hold it while playing (or mount it where it will be later).
  3. Look at the live view → “Capture”. Then throw the dice again, change the position slightly, capture again.
  4. About 10 captures: some close, some medium, flatter and steeper (but at least about 30° from above).
  5. For each image, tell below the enlarged dice: readable / unsure / not readable - can you say which symbol is on top? Everything counts: colour, kind (pips or figure) and drawing.
  6. Optional: “AI assessment” - Claude tells what it recognises (second opinion).
  7. Check the summary at the bottom → “Apply”.

Pay attention to:

  • With the camera board, not with the phone - different lens, different field of view → the pixel sizes would be wrong.
  • The whole tray should be in the picture if possible (then the angle estimate is right).
  • Normal playing light, no flash, no extra lighting - it should be realistic.
  • Feel free to capture a few “bad” positions too: the limits are what is interesting.

What the trainer measures (without AI): felt = everything green, dice = bright, non-green spots on the felt. From the ellipse of the round tray it estimates the elevation (yellow line), with the tray diameter also the distance. The enlarged dice are deliberately pixelated - so you see the real resolution.

Typical problems: dice are not found → mode “Switch view”, click the image until “mask”: green = recognised as surface, red = dice. If the colour does not fit (other cloth, weak light): mode “Pick surface colour” and click once into the cloth. If two dice touch, they become one spot (orange) - then choose “no die / several” for that spot and drag the dice one by one in mode “Draw dice” (turquoise).

Sharpness number: the larger, the sharper. It has no fixed limit but is good for comparing: same scene, other light or other focus → if the number rises, it is better.

Exposure: time gain

Are the white dice overexposed? Then take an exposure series (do not move dice and camera meanwhile) and rate the 5 images - the summary then shows “per exposure”. With fixed the camera no longer adjusts: time in sensor lines (the value depends on the sensor and resolution), gain 0…30 (more = brighter, but more noise). The values the auto exposure chose are shown for each photo (“actual”).

Higher resolutions are there to compare: rate the dice per resolution, the summary then shows whether it is worth it. The live view continues in the last chosen resolution (choppier at 1600 × 1200).
